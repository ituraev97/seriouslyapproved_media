## Quotes and where they are (all from the description / specification unless noted)
### US 6,281 — Dress-pin (scan p1)
- "a faithful and accurate description" (spoken only)
- "a pin made of one piece of wire or metal combining a spring, and clasp or catch"
- "the point of said pin is forced and by its own spring securely retained" (paraphrased: "held by its own spring")
- "They may be made of common pin wire, or of the precious metals."
- "there being no joint to break or pivot to wear or get loose as in other plans"
- "Any ornamental design may be attached to the bar C" (spoken as "…to the bar")
- Facts: Fig. 1 pin A, coiled spring B, catch D at the end of bar C; Fig. 2 pin detached from the catch "and thrown open by the spring B"; Figs. 6–8 ornamental designs.

### US 188,292 — Ear-mufflers (scan p1)
- "by which a simpler and more effective device of this character may be produced than is now in ordinary use"
- "a flattened metallic spring, semicircular in shape" (spoken)
- "acting as a stop to prevent the lower portion of the pad from swinging outwardly too far" (paraphrased; Google has "acting")
- "may be also lined or covered with fur, wool, skin, or any other fibrous non-conductor of heat" (frame "covered with cloth")
- "Fig. 2, a view representing the muffler in use"; "the body A is passed over the top of the head of the wearer"; "its contractile action causing the pads F to press firmly against the ears, and protect them from cold"
- "in a manner which will be readily obvious to all conversant with such matters" (Google OCR: "mannerwhich")
- Facts: pad frame = single wire bent into an oval, works in a socket at the spring's end.

### US 375,962 — Artificial straw (scan p1)
- "to provide a cheap, durable, and unobjectionable substitute for the natural straws" (continues "commonly used for the administration of medicines, beverages, &c.")
- "colored in imitation of the natural straw"
- "I dip the same into a vat of molten paraffine" (Google OCR "paral fine"; spoken "dipped into a vat of molten paraffine")
- "an imitation straw requiring close examination in order to distinguish it from the natural straw"
- Facts: Manila paper strip (Fig. 2), rotating spindle B with slits (Fig. 3), winding obliquely/spirally (Fig. 4), paste at corner e.

### US 618,428 — Candy-machine (scan p2–p3)
- "to obtain an edible product consisting of the said filaments of melted and spun sugar or candy"
- "thus forming into silk-like filaments and small beads of candy"
- "propelled by foot-power in the manner of a lathe" (treadle W)
- "several different forms or proportions of filaments, beads, or solid bands of candy" ("By regulating the heat…")
- Facts: rotating pan A with serrated cover C (gaps g g), burner m below, gas from "the gasolene-tank R", air pump Q; fender E "receives particles of melted sugar or candy" and encloses the filaments.

### US 889,823 — Carpet sweeper and cleaner (scan p1–p2)
- "the wires 24 will be automatically wound upon the reel when it becomes slack"
- "which in use may be connected to a common incandescent lamp socket in the usual manner"
- Facts: electric motor 2 "preferably to the top" of casing 1; fan blades 5 create suction; brush 12 in front housing 18; dust "conveyed from the fan casing into the sack 27, which sack is supported in elevation by the handle 20" (Google OCR artifacts); traveling wheels 34.

### US 1,185,124 — Disappearing bed (scan p1–p2)
- "beds which are adapted to be moved into a closet or recess when not in use"
- "so that they may be used in either chamber on opposite sides of the wall"
- "thereby preventing rattling as the door is moved"
- "the bed is swung quicker than the door"
- Facts: Fig. 1 top view, bed folded upward, positions on both sides of the wall (dotted lines); Fig. 2 vertical section folded; arm 14, slide 15, spring 17 connect bed and door.

### US 1,505,592 — Frozen confectionery (scan p1–p3)
- "which can be conveniently consumed without contamination by contact with the hand" … "and without the need for a plate, spoon, fork or other implement"
- "small containers which may be ordinary test tubes are charged with the liquid syrup"
- "It will be noted that a substantial tensile force is required to withdraw the confection" ("…operates as a relatively air-tight piston … forming substantially a vacuum therebehind")
- "bass, birch and poplar being found most suitable" (Google OCR "blrch", spoken only); "relatively porous though sapless and, therefore, tasteless wood"
- "The wooden handle is an insulator of heat"
- "the confection may be consumed by sucking thereon" (paraphrased) … "as a refreshing drink derived from the solid on the stick, leaving a white and colorless mass of ice or snow" (scan OCR "show"; Google "snow")
- Facts: board 14 presses the sticks down against buoyancy (Fig. 2); container loosened by immersion in lukewarm water.

### US 1,706,491 — Combined dipping, cooking, and article-holding apparatus (scan p2–p4)
- "wieners, boiled ham, hard boiled eggs, cheese, sliced peaches, pineapples, bananas and like fruit, and cherries, dates, figs, strawberries" (continues "etc., when impaled on sticks and dipped in a batter")
- "a new and novel edible food product"
- "a clean, wholesome and tasty refreshment"
- "to hold the articles in proximity to the surface of the liquid so as to be kept warm until served" (spoken "to be kept warm until served")
- "For the purposes of retaining heat, confining odors, preventing oil from spattering" (cover 25; spoken paraphrase)
- "thoroughly cooked in about one and one-half minutes"; "the crust is crispy and well browned"; "that the amount of oil absorbed by the batter is practically nil"
- "if the article is fruit the flavor is not destroyed but rather the juice of the fruit flavors the surrounding pastry"
- "the general appearance of the food product is that of a cruller on a stick"
- Facts: clips 12 on rim 7 hold sticks 8 upright, food under the oil (Fig. 4); stick laid horizontally on the clip holds food above the oil (Fig. 9); cover 25 (Fig. 8).

## "Did it work?" facts (web sources)
- Safety pin: "he owed someone a $15 debt"; "Hunt sells rights to it for just $400" — History.com, https://history.com/this-day-in-history/safety-pin-patented-walter-hunt (spoken as "the story goes").
- Earmuffs: idea at 15; factory near his hometown; "In its best year, 1936, the earmuff factory produced over 400,000 pairs"; Chester Greenwood Day each December; "It wasn't the first earmuff. It was an improved earmuff." — Smithsonian Magazine, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/chester-greenwood-invented-earmuffs-or-did-he-180962434/
- Straw: rye grass straws "imparted an undesirable grassy flavor"; factory produced cigarette holders; "By 1890, Stone's factory was producing more drinking straws than cigarette holders." — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Stone
- Cotton candy: "dentist William Morrison and confectioner John C. Wharton"; 1904 World's Fair as "Fairy Floss", "selling 68,655 boxes at 25¢" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_candy
- Vacuum: janitor at Zollinger's Dry Goods Store, Canton; pillow case borrowed from the store as the dirt bag; broom stick handle; showed it to cousin Susan Hoover; her husband William Henry Hoover, a leather-goods manufacturer, bought the patent in 1908; "most people today associate the vacuum cleaner with him" — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Spangler
- Murphy bed: legend — wooing an opera singer while living in a one-room apartment in San Francisco; 1989, U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit ruled "Murphy bed" generic — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murphy_bed
- Popsicle: Epperson sold the rights to the Joe Lowe Co. in the 1920s and later said he regretted it; "some 2 billion Popsicles are sold each year"; origin story "somewhat apocryphal" — NPR (via NHPR), https://www.nhpr.org/npr-blogs/2015-07-22/how-an-11-year-old-boy-invented-the-popsicle
- Corn dog: Wikipedia cites US 1,706,491 (filed 1927, granted 1929) in the corn dog's history; Carl and Neil Fletcher claim they introduced "Corny Dogs" at the State Fair of Texas between 1938 and 1942 — Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_dog
