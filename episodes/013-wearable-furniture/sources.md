## 1 · US 5,927,797 — Portable work stool
- Filed Aug. 6, 1998 · issued July 27, 1999. https://patents.google.com/patent/US5927797
- Scan: `pat/5927797` (p1–p2 drawing sheets Figs. 1–6; p0 front page not shown).
- Quotes (all Google verbatim):
  - "Shoeing a horse typically takes about one hour" (Background)
  - "field work, milking, working on vehicles" (Background)
  - "a person weighing between about 105 and 245 pounds" (Description)
  - "If the horse is moving the farrier vertically for more than nine inches, the horse is most likely out of control" + "and the farrier had better move away from the horse." (Description)
- Facts: for farriers; the farrier "typically crouches with no support and grips the lower portion of the horse's or animal's leg between his legs" (Background); seat strapped to the waist with straps 26; front strap 28 locates the seat between the legs ("the user merely needs to move the seat of the stool into position by using the front strap" — Google verbatim); single telescoping leg, pin/bolt in holes sets height in increments of about one inch; coil spring keeps lower end in contact with the ground; for horse shoeing the length is about 29 to 36 inches; preferred spring travel four to seven inches, "nine inches of vertical movement is sufficient".
