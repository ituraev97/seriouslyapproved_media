# FILE 012 — DID IT WORK? — 8 Weird Kitchen Gadgets That Got Real Patents. Did They Work?
Scans: archive.org USPTO disks (pat/<n>/, not committed). Quotes checked against Google Patents text (WebFetch, 2026-11-12); OCR typos in Google's text noted. Front pages are not shown (old patents: drawings sheets only, signatures blanked; 1975/1998: drawing sheets 1–2 only).

## 1. US 506,207 — Peanut-roaster or corn-popper
- Inventor: Charles Cretors, Chicago, Illinois. Filed Aug. 10, 1891 (Serial No. 402,259). Issued Oct. 10, 1893.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US506207 · archive.org: us_patent_00498174_to_RE011396_016 (USP016…/00506207.tif)
- Figures: pop1 = Fig. 1 (sheet 1, p0).
- Quotes (description):
  - "so that a rocking movement of the bar will depress the rod or cord m … which latter is attached to the arm or other movable part of a figure N … and actuates same for the purpose of attracting the attention of people passing the device."
  - "This tank and the water therein, are heated by the exhaust steam from the engine"
  - "The engine … which actuates all the rotating parts of the apparatus is situated on a bed plate"
  - "This pan is made of sheet metal with a wire gauze top or band for preventing the popped corn from flying therefrom"
  - stirrer shaft: "is located in close proximity to the bottom of the pan and is designed to prevent the corn from burning."
  - "while the front and two sides thereof are of glass."
  - boiler: "The casing or frame A extends upwardly at its rear end, in curved form for the reception of the boiler"
- Did it work: Wikipedia "Popcorn maker" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popcorn_maker): US 506,207 applied Aug. 10, 1891, granted Oct. 10, 1893; Cretors took his popcorn wagon and peanut roaster to the midway of the 1893 Columbian Exposition in Chicago. cretors.com "Our History" (https://www.cretors.com/our-history-revised): "the perfect stage for Charles Cretors to debut his revolutionary steam-powered popcorn machine"; the moving figure = the "Tosty Rosty Man", a steam-powered clown, to attract attention; Andrew G. Cretors became the fifth company president in 2006.

## 2. US 514,200 — Capped-bottle opener
- Inventor: William Painter, Baltimore, Maryland, assignor to the Crown Cork and Seal Company. Filed June 5, 1893 (Serial No. 476,638). Issued Feb. 6, 1894.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US514200 · archive.org: us_patent_00498174_to_RE011396_016
- Figures: open1 = Figs. 1–7 (sheet, p0).
- Quotes (description):
  - "specially adapted for the removal of hard metal sealing caps from bottles having locking shoulders"
  - "My bottle opener essentially embodies a handle, having at one end thereof, a cap centering gage, and also a cap engaging lip" (Google OCR: "acap")
  - "the gage will assure an appropriate bearing or fulcrum on top of the cap"
  - "so that when the handle is raised as in Fig. 5, the cap will be pried from the bottle"
  - Fig. 7: "Fig. 7, in two views, illustrates one of my openers adapted to be mounted on a table or counter."
- Did it work: greatpatent.com (https://greatpatent.com/the-bottle-cap-that-taught-gillette-to-make-things-disposable): crown cork US 468,258 granted Feb. 2, 1892; "Crown Cork & Seal became a large and durable company"; "a traveling salesman named King Camp Gillette"; by Gillette's own account Painter told him to invent something "used once, thrown away, and bought again"; Gillette applied the idea to the disposable razor blade.

## 3. US 576,395 — Ice-cream mold and disher
- Inventor: Alfred L. Cralle, Pittsburg, Pennsylvania. Filed June 10, 1896. Issued Feb. 2, 1897.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US576395 · archive.org: us_patent_00569190_to_RE011580_019
- Figures: scoop1 = Figs. 1 & 2 (p0).
- Quotes (description): "has for its object to construct a disher that may be conveniently operated with one hand." · "The operator grasps the handles and by forcing the two together causes the segmental rack h to engage" · "with the rack k and rotate the mold a … around the cutters or blades f, which will loosen the cream" · "and allow the same to fall into the saucer or plate provided therefor." · "a represents the mold, which is shown in the drawings for the purpose of illustration as cone-shaped, though this mold may be of any desired shape." · "The cutters or blades f f are secured to rivet e and engage the inner face of the mold." · "By this construction of a disher only one hand will be required to operate the same" (Google-verified; added to t3 after the fact-check, plus the Mental Floss line added to s3 — both already-verified sources)
- Did it work: Mental Floss (https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/alfred-l-cralle-who-invented-ice-cream-scoop): worked as a hotel porter and at a drugstore in Pittsburgh; "Nearly every ice cream scoop on the market today takes inspiration from his original patent."; "there's no evidence that he ever made a fortune off his product." (His death is not mentioned in the video.)

## 4. US 1,867,377 — Machine for slicing an entire loaf of bread at a single operation
- Inventor: Otto F. Rohwedder, Davenport, Iowa; assignor to Mac-Roe Sales & Mfg. Company. Filed Nov. 26, 1928. Issued July 12, 1932.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US1867377 · archive.org: us_patent_01859046_to_01876039_083
- Figures: bread4 = Fig. 4 (sheet 2, p1: cutting bands 47); bread1 = Fig. 1 (sheet 1, p0: plan view of the delivering and receiving conveyors).
- Quotes (description): "adjacent bands move in opposite directions to prevent crushing and displacement of the" · "hold the slices in substantial loaf form until the wrapping machine places a suitable wrapper around the same" (spaced side belts on the delivery conveyor) · "the speed of slicing complete loaves of bread will be materially increased." · Fig. 1: "a plan view of the bread slicing machine showing the means for delivering the bread to the slicing apparatus and the means for receiving the sliced bread from the slicing apparatus."
- Did it work: Wikipedia "Sliced bread" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliced_bread): "The first commercial use of the machine was by the Chillicothe Baking Company" "who sold their first slices on July 7, 1928"; "Kleen Maid Sliced Bread" "proved to be a success"; Wonder Bread began marketing sliced bread nationwide in 1930; wartime ban effective Jan. 18, 1943, rescinded March 8, 1943 (≈ 7 weeks).

## 5. US 2,241,368 — Filtering device
- Inventor: Peter Schlumbohm, New York, N.Y. (one-half assigned to Isaac Hatter, Akron, Ohio). Filed April 13, 1939. Issued May 6, 1941.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US2241368 · archive.org: us_patent_02240502_to_02256052_107
- Figures: chem1 = Fig. 1, chem3 = Fig. 3 (sheet 1), chem4 = Fig. 4 (sheet 2).
- Quotes (description): "a combination of a filter funnel adapted to hold a paper filter and a bottle-like flask adapted to receive filtrate." · "the necessity for the air passage was created by the simple joining of the filter funnel to the filtrate flask." · "the deep groove 6 cooperating with the lip 5 conveniently serves as a pouring spout" · "the filter paper … which holds the spent coffee grounds is taken out of the funnel" · "The filtrate flask … is preferably constructed in the well known shape of an Erlemeyer flask" (sic; narration says Erlenmeyer) · "cleanliness is of primary significance in all filtering operations."
- Did it work: Wikipedia "Chemex" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemex): invented by Peter Schlumbohm in 1941; in the collection of the Museum of Modern Art; 1958: "one of the best-designed products of modern times"; appears in the novel From Russia with Love (breakfast scene).

- Did it work: Wikipedia "Gilhoolie" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilhoolie): US 2,669,142; filed 1952, granted Feb. 16, 1954; debuted 1953; for several years sold by mail order by the Riswell Company (Cos Cob, Connecticut); inventor described as a retired dentist from Yonkers (Wikipedia says "Dr. Charles W. Fuller" — the video does not name him); his only kitchen-device patent. Vermont Country Store (https://www.vermontcountrystore.com/gilhoolie-jar-opener/product/66578), item #66578, $19.95: "The original Gilhoolie is back."

## 7. US 3,885,321 — Domestic appliance for drying vegetables
- Inventor: Gilberte Fouineteau, Gray, France. Filed Oct. 25, 1973. Issued May 27, 1975. No copyright notice in the text.
- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US3885321 (all quotes confirmed) · also https://uspto.report/patent/grant/3885321 · archive.org: us_patent_03875075_to_03889615_218
- Figures: spin1 = Fig. 1 (sheet 1, p1), spin2 = Fig. 2 (sheet 2, p2).
- Quotes (description): "the draining of vegetables and salads under the action of centrifugal force" · "the basket has a central shaft which rises to its upper opening" · "It is impossible to place the basket on its bottom and to use it alone with its opening turned upwards." · "forms with a handle or knob 11 carried near its edge a crank for rotating the disc 6." · "Thus, the basket is easily removable and usable as such, for example as a strainer, outside the appliance." · claim 1: "a removable vegetable basket which has a bottom provided with a recess to receive said pivot"
- Did it work: Wikipedia "Salad spinner" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salad_spinner): Gilberte Fouineteau credited with the modern salad spinner (US3885321; removable basket, no central post); Mouli Manufacturing introduced a crank-operated spinner to the American market in 1974; critics called it "another gourmet gadget"; an estimated 500,000 units sold in 1978 alone.

- Google Patents: https://patents.google.com/patent/US5840189 · archive.org: us_patent_05839689_to_05848652_403
- Figures: kcup1 = Fig. 1 (sheet 1, p1), kcup4 = Fig. 4 (sheet 2, p2). Front page not shown.
- Quotes (description): "the coffee is exposed to the air where it can easily get stale or contaminated" · "hermetically sealed for freshness and against contamination" · "it is standard practice to replace the oxygen in the package with nitrogen." · "Even when the filter 14 is wetted, it will not collapse or sag against the inner wall 12a of the base 12." · "The cover 16 is yieldably pierceable with an instrument such as a tubular needle 42" · "The base 12 is yieldably pierceable with an instrument such as a needle 22"
