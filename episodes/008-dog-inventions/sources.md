## Quotes and facts per patent
### US 5,003,756 — Dog garment with flea repelling means (Sheet 1, Figs. 1–2)
- "These collars appear to be successful in keeping the fleas off the dog's back and head" — description (background: flea collars)
- "the fleas merely migrate to other portions of the dog" — description
- "particularly to those portions where the dog has difficulty in scratching or biting." — description
- "if a flea survives by migrating to a distant point of the dog, the dog can reach him by scratching or biting." — description
- Facts: a coat over chest, neck, sides and back; inside, rows of loops hold commercial flea strips (Fig. 2). Fig. 1 = dog wearing the coat.

### US 887,072 — Leading device (p0, Fig. 1)
- "a device for leading pet dogs or other pet animals when they are taken out to accompany a person in his or her walks" — description, opening
- "The invention is particularly adapted for ladies" — description (period phrasing, framed as such)
- "running on the wrong side of lamp posts or pedestrians" — description (Google text has OCR typo "diflicult" earlier in that sentence → only this fragment quoted)
- "thus causing much annoyance to the owner" — description
- "The objects of the present invention are to obviate and overcome all these difficulties and annoyances due to the usual form of leash" — description
- "frequently the leash is dropped and the dog permitted to run away." — description
- "which is an awkward performance when obliged to be done frequently." — description (wrapping the cord around the hand)
- Facts: a casing strapped onto the dog collar holds a spring drum; the chain/strap leash winds in and out of it; handle I at the hand end. Fig. 1 = collar with the device. Filed Jan. 1907, issued May 12, 1908 → an early self-winding leash (description: "prevent the leash from becoming tangled as the dog runs about").

### US 3,272,181 — Animal actuated drinking fountain (Sheet 1, Figs. 1–2)
- Spoken paraphrase (not on screen as quote): the fountain doubles as a training device ("press here for water" = channel joke).
- "The invention is specially designed for use by dogs, both as a means to obtain water and as a training device." — description
- "for allowing animals to operate the drinking fountain by a conscious applying of weight thereto." — description
- "A further object of the present invention is to provide a drinking fountain which may be used as a training means for dogs." — description
- Facts: bowl with an elongated stem; the dog steps on a pedal/plate, a valve opens and water comes out of the stem (Fig. 2 "showing the fountain being used by a dog"). The patent heading: inventor "assignor of five percent to" another person (spoken: "assigned five percent", without names).

### US 4,134,364 — Dog exercising device (Sheet 1, Fig. 1)
- "which positions the dog alongside the bicycle" — description
- "prevents the dog from running into the vehicle or running off sidewise, endangering the rider." — description
- "which yieldably resists normal tugging by the dog" — description
- "releases if the dog should suddenly lurch with great force" — description
- "which is preferably an adult tricycle" — description (FIG. 1 "is a side elevational view of an adult tricycle with the invention applied thereto")
- Spoken paraphrase: "a novel attachment which is easy to apply and remove and which has an adjustable tether for different size animals" — description
- Facts: a stand-off bar clamped to the frame holds the leash out to the side; rubber cushions; FIG. 5 = dual harness arrangement (two dogs).

### US 2,438,979 — Frame and harness for dog baths (p0, Figs. 1–2)
- "a device for holding an animal such as a dog in a fixed position in a bath tub" — description
- "to wash the dog without any interference on the part of the latter" — description (Google capitalises "Wash")
- "without causing any particular discomfort to the animal" — description
- "showing a dog harnessed for being washed" — description (Fig. 1)
- Spoken: "Anyone who has washed a dog knows why" / "The latter being the dog" = channel commentary.
- Facts: rectangular frame of bars wedged across the tub by screws with rubber caps; three straps: collar (20), trunk near the front legs (21), near the hind legs (22); frame can be taken apart "for storing in a small space"; harness also prevents "the dog from shaking itself until it is thoroughly wiped after being washed" (spoken: "No shaking."); adjustable to tubs of different width. 7 claims.

### US 4,995,374 — Throw and fetch doggie toy (Sheet 1, Figs. 1–3)
- "is an activity which is probably as old as the man-dog relationship." — description (throw and fetch)
- "the human owner usually wears out long before the dog is ready to call it enough for the day." — description
- "at least some dogs never seem to get enough of this throw and fetch game" — description
- "It is yet a further object of this invention to provide a doggie toy which automatically exercises a dog." — description
- Facts: box with a launch tube fired by a solenoid; the dog drops the ball into a hopper on top, it rolls into the tube, a sensor waits for it, the box launches it; Figs. 1–3 = launch, chase/return, drop-in. NOT used: lines about owners' "arm ailments"/"physical limitations".

### US 528,066 — Tread-wheel (p0, Figs. 1–3; p1 text)
- "to be operated by the gravity or weight of an animal, as a dog, goat or sheep" — description, opening
- "a wheel that is strong, light of weight and easily operated" — description (object of the invention)
- "a milk or cream separator, a churn, a pump, a sewing machine, a washing machine, a printing press" — description (Google text: same)
- "provides sufficient space for the convenient travel therein of the dog or other animal that runs the wheel." — description
- "The inner surface of the rim serves as a continuous track" — description (Google OCR: "thc rim" — on-screen quote uses the scan's spelling "the rim"; fact-checker to confirm or drop)
- Facts: the animal treads "continuously upwardly on the inside of the rim"; a belt on the outer rim surface carries the power; smaller pulley M for "greater power and less rapidity"; a governor (weighted lever / tumbling-bob, Fig. 5) regulates speed; a pole holds the animal's strap (sentence not quoted: Google OCR "bitching"). One half assigned to a second person (no names). No animal drawn.

### US 5,918,611 — Animal umbrella (Sheet 1, Figs. 1, 2, 4)
- "Animals as well as humans require protection from the rain when they are being walked." — description
- "It is not a simple task to have the walker hold an umbrella both over himself and the animal he is walking." — description
- "STILL YET ANOTHER OBJECT of the present invention is to provide an animal umbrella that is self-supporting." — description
- "FINALLY, STILL YET ANOTHER EXAMPLE" — description, Description of the Prior Art (opening words of the last prior-art paragraph)
- "an animal umbrella that is in the form of a cap with pointed ears extending outwardly therefrom" — description (prior art)
- Facts: saddle clips onto an ordinary harness and collar; a shank rises to a foldable canopy with a drape; Fig. 1 side view, Fig. 2 rear view, Fig. 4 canopy. The prior art includes the same inventor's earlier animal umbrella, US 5,546,970 (spoken: "an earlier animal umbrella patent", no name).

### US 5,732,415 — Adjustable pet eyewear (Sheet 2, Figs. 3 and 5)
- "pets, and primarily dogs, who enjoy hanging their heads out of the windows of moving cars." — description
- "Perhaps this is because they enjoy the feel of the wind against their skin." — description
- "Perhaps it is simply that they like to look outward at the passing countryside." — description
- "The world may never know." — description
- "pug nosed dogs will be fitted just as easily as long-nosed dogs, such as the one shown in the figures." — description
- "It is, therefore, an object of the invention to protect the eyes of animals, and particularly canines." — description
- Fig. 3 = alternate embodiment: one-piece transparent goggle (44) worn by a dog (spoken: "a clear goggle"). Fig. 4/5 = two-piece version.
- Facts: two lens units joined by an adjustable bridge; straps to a neck collar; a pivot lets them flip up onto the head (Fig. 5); Fig. 3 dog wearing them.

### US 4,178,742 — Protective animal goggles (Sheet 1, Fig. 2) — callback inside #1
- "As instinct commands, the animal desires to extend his head out an open window and into the onrushing wind." — description
- "The device is especially suitable for dogs and particularly for breeds such as poodles, schnauzers, etc." — description
- Facts: issued Dec. 18, 1979 (spoken: "Back in nineteen seventy-nine"); US 5,732,415 issued 1998; Fig. 2 front view of a schnauzer-type dog in the goggles.
