## 1. US 2,953,394: "Occupant Propelled Gyral Wheel" (the human hamster wheel)
- Filed Feb. 6, 1958. Issued Sept. 20, 1960. Inventor Everett G. Anderson, Sioux City, Iowa.
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US2953394
- Disk: us_patent_02941831_to_02955747_154 (3 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the single drawing sheet (pre-1971, so it can be shown). p1 and p2 are text.
- Best figure: **p0, Fig. 1**, a side view of a man in shorts standing upright inside a giant bicycle-tyred wheel. His hands grip overhead handles and his feet are strapped into saddles at the bottom. It reads instantly. Fig. 2 is an end view of the wheel. Figs. 3–4 are cross-sections of the roller hand grips and foot saddles.
- Quotes:
  1. "My invention relates to what I refer to as a gyral wheel that is designed for use as entertainment and for exercise by both children and adults." (description, col. 1, first sentence)
  2. "In using this vehicle, an occupant, indicated for illustration at 38, will be in an upright standing position and will maintain such position while propelling the wheel 10." (col. 1)
  3. "...the occupant by shifting his weight from leg to leg and moving both arms and legs in piston like fashion in various degrees of synchronization as will become apparent to him will cause the wheel 10 to be propelled." (col. 2)
  4. "As wheel 10 rotates the occupant becomes in effect an axis connected in a vertical plane across the vehicle by extensible and retractable connecting means represented by his arms and legs." (col. 2)
  5. "...by suitable practice will be able to propel the vehicle for his enjoyment, sport and exercise." (col. 2)
  6. "An occupant propelled vehicle, comprising a single open center ground engaging wheel," (claim 1, opening)
- How it works:
  - A single open-centre wheel with a bicycle-type tyre. Two elliptical frames are fixed to it by spokes, one on each side.
  - A hand grip and a foot saddle ride on rollers in channel tracks inside each frame. The grips stay overhead and the feet stay at the bottom.
  - The rider pushes off the ground with one foot, then straps both feet into the saddles.
  - The rider rolls the wheel by shifting weight from leg to leg and pumping arms and legs "in piston like fashion".
- Safety: a person playing inside a wheel for fun and exercise. No injury, medical or risk language. "Children" appears only as intended users, so the script can say "adults" or quote the sentence as written.

## 2. US 4,457,084: "Hopping and Dancing Shoes" (spring shoes for disco)
- Filed Apr. 8, 1981. Issued July 3, 1984. Inventors Horibata, Sondreal and Chu.
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4457084
- Disk: us_patent_04455900_to_04469033_263 (5 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the front page (do not show). p1 is drawing sheet 1. p2 is drawing sheet 2. p3 and p4 are text.
- Best figures:
  - **p1, Fig. 1**: a lace-up shoe on two big coil springs above a base plate.
  - **p2, Fig. 9**: a shoe on large conical springs with bells (94, 94') hanging on the side. This is the funniest single image.
  - Other figures: p1 Figs. 3–5 are variants with a skirt and moulded springs. p2 Fig. 7 is a strap-on spring sole. p2 Fig. 8 has an arch "stimulating member".
- Quotes:
  1. "The present hopping and dancing shoes may be used by middle aged men for weight control exercise..." (description, col. 3)
  2. "The hopping and dancing shoe 91 is suitable to accompany music such as rock n' roll, a flamenco or disco..." (col. 3)
  3. "Bells 94, 94' can be attached to the sides of the shoe 91 or on its shoe lace." (col. 2)
  4. "The excitement and stimulation of player's arch is amplified with cushioning of a spring 83..." (col. 2)
  5. "The player also recovers his physical fatigue while hopping and dancing because of the above mentioned stimulating..." (col. 3)
  6. "A rhythm source H, such as castanets 94, 94' as shown in FIG. 9 are attached to either the sole member, the shoe 1, or shoe laces to create a desired rhythm energized by the vibration or rhythmical movement of the player's foot." (col. 2, from the OCR)
- How it works:
  - Coil springs connect the shoe's sole to a separate stable base plate.
  - The outer coils touch the sole and the base so the shoe resists twisting.
  - Castanets or bells can be clipped to the shoe so your hopping makes the rhythm.
  - A rubber skirt can cover the springs to keep grass and wood out.
  - Another version straps a removable spring sole onto an ordinary shoe.
- Safety: a fitness and dance novelty. **Do not use** the opening sentences, which talk about twisted ankles and injury. The quotes above avoid them.

## 3. US 5,989,168: "Exercise Bed" (the bed that bends you)
- Filed Dec. 30, 1998. Issued Nov. 23, 1999. Inventor Long-Tan See, Taiwan.
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US5989168
- Disk: us_patent_05981641_to_05991586_419 (5 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the front page (do not show). p1 is Fig. 1. p2 is Figs. 2A–2C. p3 is Fig. 3. p4 is text.
- Best figure: **p3, Fig. 3**. A person lies under a sheet with their feet clamped between padded rollers at the end of the bed, and the middle of the bed bulges up under their back. The page is drawn sideways, so rotate it 90°. Fig. 1 is the empty bed frame with three sets of foot rollers. Fig. 2A is a side view with the motor. Figs. 2B and 2C show the rollers and support frames in action.
- Quotes:
  1. "The conventional exercise machines can not be used as a bed for sleeping or resting on." (background)
  2. "The conventional beds can not be used as an exercise device for relaxing or training the muscles of a person." (background)
  3. "It is therefore practical and economical to have an exercise bed which can be used as an exercise device as well as a bed for a person to sleep or rest on." (background)
  4. "As the motor is started, both front bed frame and the rear bed frame are displaced upwards to push the back muscles of a person resting on the exercise bed." (summary)
  5. "The backbone A2 of the user A of the exercise bed of the present invention is therefore braced and extended." (detailed description)
- How it works:
  - The bed is made in two hinged halves. A transmission motor sits under the rear half.
  - Three sets of soft rollers at the foot end hold the user's feet and soles, and the rollers are raised or lowered with a hand wheel.
  - The motor's rod pushes a movable frame, which pulls an elastic cord, which drags the front half's wheels toward the rear.
  - That makes the hinge in the middle of the bed bulge upward under the user's back.
- Safety: a home bed-and-exercise device for "relaxing or training the muscles". No patients, therapy or injury language. Keep "backbone… braced and extended" framed as stretching, not treatment.

## 4. US 4,512,567: "Exercise Bicycle Apparatus Particularly Adapted for Controlling Video Games" (1983 exergaming)
- Filed Mar. 28, 1983. Issued Apr. 23, 1985 (issue date from the scan's front page; the Google Patents page did not show it). Inventor Robert V. Phillips, N.J.
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4512567
- Disk: us_patent_04506342_to_04518907_267 (8 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the front page (do not show). p1 is drawing sheet 1. p2 is drawing sheet 2. p3–p7 are text.
- Best figure: **p2, Fig. 2**, a clean perspective drawing of an exercise bike with a cable running "TO VIDEO GAME". No person is drawn. **p1, Fig. 1**, a block diagram of bike, paddle inputs, video game and TV, is a good second shot. Other figures: p1 Figs. 6–8 are the interface box and circuits. p2 Figs. 3–5 are the gear knob, bracket and potentiometer.
- Quotes:
  1. "As one can ascertain, video games have become extremely popular and are played by many people, particularly those in their teenage years." (background, col. 1)
  2. "It is basically an object of the present invention to devise an exercise bicycle which has apparatus incorporated therein to enable one to fully control and play any type of video game while performing worthwhile exercise." (col. 1)
  3. "Hence in order to watch television, one must operate the generator by means of the bicycle, and if one does not do so, one will not be able to view the television." (col. 1, describing earlier patent 4,298,893)
  4. "Accordingly, the user of such a bicycle can now receive enjoyment in playing the game and exercise at the same time." (description)
  5. "Hence this apparatus is particularly useful in providing indoor exercise and in making video game playing a more healthful event." (description)
  6. "If, however, the operator pedals the bicycle rapidly enough, then he will exceed the reference voltage..." (description, Fig. 8 circuit)
- How it works:
  - A stationary exercise bike is wired into the "paddle" and switch inputs of a home video game console. The patent names the Atari 400 and 800.
  - Turning the handlebars moves a potentiometer, which steers the on-screen object.
  - Pedalling speed produces a signal proportional to wheel speed. The faster you pedal, the more you control.
  - In the switch version, pedalling fast enough to pass a reference voltage triggers a game switch.
- Safety: fitness plus home gaming. **Do not use** the sentence about video games and children's health, or the col. 3 mention of "electrical guns" in games. The quotes above avoid both. The drawing has no person, so it is weaker for a Short.

## Backup A: US 5,207,628: "Suspending Fold-Away Bed Exercising Device"
- Filed Nov. 18, 1991. Issued May 4, 1993. Inventor Otha O. Graham, Maysville, N.C.
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US5207628
- Disk: us_patent_05186229_to_05207845_332 (16 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the front page. p1–p9 are drawing sheets 1–9. p10–p15 are text.
- Best figure: **p7, Fig. 16**. A woman with big 1980s hair lies face-up in bed with her feet hooked under a padded bar and holds a pull bar. Because the view is from above, she looks like she is standing up in the bed. Very readable and odd. The other sheets are brackets, hinges and the fold-away arm.
- Quotes:
  1. "Most people, especially women, are sensitive about the appearance of their beds when not in use, therefore, exercising devices sitting in full view while not being used would be objectionable to these persons." (description, prior art). Use with care: it is a gender generalisation.
  2. "Heretofore bed exercising devices were cumbersome and inconvenient in that one or more were limited to one type of bed, unbecoming to the bed room, ..." (description, prior art)
  3. "It is another object to provide an exercising device that does not interfere with changing bed linen and does not have to be removed when making up the bed." (objects)
  4. "...shows a person P lying face up on the bed demonstrating the invention's intended use by the person for performing sit-ups while in bed." (description of Fig. 16)
  5. "Also, the invention can be used under the bed covering, and can be left there out of sight when not in use." (description)
- How it works: a padded foot bar hangs above the mattress from a U-shaped bracket mounted at the side of the bed. You hook your feet under it to do sit-ups in bed. When you are done, it folds out of sight under the bed without tools or lifting.
- Safety: sit-ups in bed for general fitness. No medical or invalid framing.

## Backup B: US 1,790,653: "Exercising Device" (the wobbling saddle, 1931)
- Filed June 7, 1928. Issued Feb. 3, 1931. Inventor William C. Brumder, deceased; Thekla U. Brumder, executrix, Milwaukee. Google Patents titles the page "Thekla u", but the official title is "Exercising Device".
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US1790653
- Disk: us_patent_01790246_to_01807929_079 (3 pages)
- Pages: p0 is the drawing sheet (pre-1971, so it can be shown). p1 and p2 are text.
- Figures on p0: Fig. 1 is a side view of an electric-motor-driven post with a saddle on top and a foot-pedal rheostat. Fig. 2 is a plan view. Fig. 3 is a front view. Fig. 4 is a section through the saddle. No person is drawn.
- Quotes:
  1. "As the shaft turns, the cam disk 12, mounted at an oblique angle on its upper end, causes the saddle to wabble, the movement being in a circumferential path, as indicated by the dotted lines in the drawing." (col. 1)
  2. "It is a further object of this invention to provide an exercising device embodying a mechanical movement which is transmitted to a saddle of the exercising machine to exercise the hip and back muscles of the user and to benefit the body generally." (col. 1)
  3. "When the device is thus equipped, the user stands against the massaging member, and the latter, by its peculiar movement, will thoroughly massage the body." (col. 1–2)
  4. "...a movable horizontally positioned saddle member engaged by said disk, the latter, when rotated, imparting a wabbling movement to the movable saddle member..." (claim 1)
- How it works: an electric motor drives a belt that turns a vertical shaft. A cam disk set at a slant on top of the shaft makes the saddle wobble in a circle. A pin in a slot stops the saddle from spinning. A foot pedal controls the speed. You sit on it, or swap the saddle for a massaging pad and lean against it.
- Safety: a general fitness and reducing machine. **Avoid** the "correcting many disorders" sentence, which sounds medical.
