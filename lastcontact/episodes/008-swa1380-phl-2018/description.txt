April 17, 2018: Southwest Airlines flight 1380, a Boeing 737-700, loses its left engine at 32,648 feet over Pennsylvania. A fan blade has separated, and parts of the engine's cover break away. One strikes the fuselage beside a cabin window, and the cabin depressurizes. About 17 minutes after the engine failure, the airplane lands in Philadelphia. One passenger died; eight others had minor injuries. This video walks through what the NTSB found, second by second, using only the NTSB's own final report (NTSB/AAR-19/03) and its public docket.

NTSB probable cause (quoted from the report, adopted November 19, 2019): "...a low-cycle fatigue crack in the dovetail of fan blade No. 13, which resulted in the fan blade separating in flight and impacting the engine fan case at a location that was critical to the structural integrity and performance of the fan cowl structure. This impact led to the in-flight separation of fan cowl components, including the inboard fan cowl aft latch keeper, which struck the fuselage near a cabin window and caused the window to depart from the airplane, the cabin to rapidly depressurize, and the passenger fatality."
