NTSB FINDING 24
“The captain’s passenger safety briefing contributed to the survival of two passengers.”
NTSB PROBABLE CAUSE · ADOPTED APRIL 6, 2010
“…the operator’s inadequate maintenance of the airplane’s tires, which resulted in multiple tire failures during takeoff roll due to severe underinflation, and the captain’s execution of a rejected takeoff (RTO) after V1, which was inconsistent with her training and standard operating procedures.”
CONTRIBUTING TO THE ACCIDENT · NTSB
1 Deficiencies in Learjet’s design of and the FAA’s certification of the thrust reverser system, which increased the severity of the accident
2 The inadequacy of Learjet’s safety analysis and the FAA’s review of it after a 2001 uncommanded forward thrust accident
3 Inadequate industry training standards for tire failure scenarios
4 The flight crew’s poor crew resource management
NTSB: uncommanded forward thrust increased the severity · whether it could have stopped without it: cannot be determined

WHAT CHANGED · NTSB SEC. 1.18, 4
OCT 2008 Learjet advisory wire AW 32-045: tire inflation can’t be judged visually
FEB 2009 FAA SAFO 09005 “Dangers of Improperly Inflated Tires”
MAR 2009 Learjet 60 flight manual: tire check within 96 hours · “absence of any [TR] lights indicates forward thrust”
2009–10 NTSB recs: reverser levers must match reversers (A-09-55/56) · tire checks (A-10-47) · tire pressure monitoring (A-10-50)
2010 NTSB recs: Part 135 experience in type (A-10-57/58) · realistic tire tests (A-10-59) · tire-failure simulator training (A-10-55/56)
