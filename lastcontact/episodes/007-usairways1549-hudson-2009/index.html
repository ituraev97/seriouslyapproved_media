NTSB PROBABLE CAUSE · ADOPTED MAY 4, 2010
“…the ingestion of large birds into each engine, which resulted in an almost total loss of thrust in both engines and the subsequent ditching on the Hudson River.”
CONTRIBUTING TO THE FUSELAGE DAMAGE AND RESULTING UNAVAILABILITY OF THE AFT SLIDE/RAFTS · NTSB
1 FAA approval of ditching certification without determining whether pilots could attain the ditching parameters without engine thrust
2 Lack of industry flight crew training and guidance on ditching techniques
3 The captain’s resulting difficulty maintaining his intended airspeed on final approach due to the task saturation resulting from the emergency situation
CONTRIBUTING TO SURVIVABILITY · NTSB
1 Flight crew decision-making and crew resource management
2 An airplane equipped for extended overwater flight (forward slide/rafts), though not required
3 Cabin crew performance in expediting the evacuation
4 Proximity of emergency responders and their immediate, appropriate response
NTSB SAFETY RECOMMENDATIONS A-10-62 … A-10-95 (34)
What the NTSB asked for
ENGINES Technology to show pilots an engine’s continuing status · lower-fan-speed flocking-bird tests · core ingestion for smaller engines
CREWS Low-altitude dual-engine-failure checklist · ditching training · alpha-protection training
CABIN Life vests + flotation cushions on all flights · better life vests · briefings
AIRPORTS Proactive wildlife hazard assessments
