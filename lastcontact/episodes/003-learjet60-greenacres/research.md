# Research: Learjet 60 crash, Greenacres FL, Nov 1, 2026 (NTSB ERA27MA026)
Compiled Nov 16, 2026. Facts only. Victims and crew are referred to by role only, with no names.
Time zone: DST ended at 2 a.m. on Sun Nov 1, 2026, so Florida afternoon times that day are **EST (UTC-5)**. Austin was on CST, so 10:37 a.m. ET = 9:37 a.m. local Austin. Sources say "ET". AIN/TWC say "EST". FAA says "local time".
Legend: [NTSB] = NTSB's own page or NTSB officials quoted by media. [FAA]. [LOCAL] = county/sheriff/FDOT. [MEDIA] = reporting only.

## A. Official core facts (NTSB investigation page)
Source for 1–9: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/ERA27MA026.aspx (fetched Nov 16, 2026; the page says "This information is preliminary and subject to change").
1. The page is titled "Crash of Learjet 60". Investigation ID ERA27MA026. Status: Ongoing. Event date: 11/1/2026. Location: Greenacres, FL. [NTSB]
2. The NTSB "launched a go team to investigate the crash of a Learjet 60 operated by Aurora Aviation dba Upper Jet." "The flight was lost from radar and radio contact while en route from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS), Austin, Texas to North Eleuthera Airport (MYEH), North Eleuthera, Bahamas." [NTSB]
3. **Update Nov 5:** Excavation at the crash site "began late yesterday" (Nov 4) and continued. It "included finding a portion of one engine." Debris was found away from the impact location "and also on the roofs of homes and buildings near the crash site." All recovered wreckage will be sent to a secure location for examination. [NTSB]
4. **Update Nov 8:** On-scene activities were completed, except the excavation of the impact site, which was still ongoing. Cleared areas: Green Acres Mobile Home Community, Reading Truck, Silver Oaks Plaza, Value Store It Self Storage and John I. Leonard High School. [NTSB]
5. **Update Nov 8:** Investigators "recovered and identified portions of the aircraft, including components from both wings, both engines, the flight deck, vertical stabilizer, horizontal stabilizer, left elevator, aileron sections and multiple flap track assemblies." Some parts were sent to the NTSB laboratories in Washington, D.C. [NTSB]
6. **Update Nov 9:** The cockpit voice recorder (CVR) was recovered. It "was located at the site of initial impact." It "is being evaluated in the NTSB Vehicle Recorders Laboratory in Washington, D.C." "The excavation work at the impact site has been completed." [NTSB]
7. Public appeal (Nov 5 and Nov 8): anyone who finds debris should not keep, move, alter or dispose of it, and should email the NTSB (witness address) with a photo. "Recovering all available wreckage is critical to the investigation." [NTSB]
8. The page lists media briefing videos: Nov 2, 2026 https://www.youtube.com/live/9utTZTQbKz8 and Nov 3, 2026 https://www.youtube.com/live/_7aLwIMptyc. Transcripts were NOT retrieved for this file. Quotes below come from media reports of these briefings. Recommendation: verify the key quotes against the videos. [NTSB]
9. NTSB photo on the page: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/PublishingImages/ERA27MA026-img1.jpg (verified, HTTP 200). Caption: "An NTSB investigator searches the impact site for wreckage and debris from the Learjet 60." The page also links to the general NTSB Flickr account (https://www.flickr.com/photos/ntsb/). ## B. FAA
11. FAA statement dated Nov 1, 2026, category "General Aviation / Cresthaven, Florida": "A Learjet 60 crashed in a residential area near Cresthaven, Florida, around 12:50 p.m. local time on Sunday, Nov. 1. Nine people were on board. The aircraft departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was headed to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas. The FAA and National Transportation Safety Board will investigate. The NTSB will be in charge of the investigation and will provide all updates." — https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/statements/accident_incidents [FAA]
12. ABC News (Nov 1) reported that the FAA said "The plane went from 35,250 feet to 8,750 feet in a little over a minute." ABC also attributed "a 22-year-old twin-engine Learjet 60" to the FAA. These figures are NOT in the FAA's published statement. They are attributed only by ABC. https://abcnews.com/US/learjet-9-aboard-crashes-palm-beach-florida-faa/story?id=137753599 [MEDIA, attributed to FAA]. This is the likely origin of the "26,000 feet in about a minute" claim (35,250 − 8,750 = 26,500 ft). No NTSB official has been found stating it.
13. FAA aircraft registry, N722BD (looked up Nov 16): Learjet Inc. model 60, **serial number 271**, status Valid, registered to an LLC (Delaware), certificate issued 01/04/2023. The registry's MFR Year field is blank. https://registry.faa.gov/AircraftInquiry/Search/NNumberResult?nNumberTxt=722BD [FAA]. Note: the NTSB page does not give the registration. N722BD as the accident aircraft comes from media (AIN, TWC, others). The serial number is from the registry and is not confirmed as the accident aircraft by the NTSB.

## C. Flight profile — as stated by NTSB officials at briefings (Member Tom Chapman; Investigator-in-Charge Timothy Monville)
14. Departure from AUS at about 10:37 a.m. ET on Sun Nov 1, with 2 crew and 7 passengers. — Chapman, Nov 2 briefing, per WPTV https://www.wptv.com/news/palm-beach-county/region-c-palm-beach-county/ntsb-news-conference-learjet-60-crash-in-palm-beach-county/11-2-26 and CBS12 https://cbs12.com/news/local/national-transportation-safety-board-share-new-details-palm-beach-county-learjet-60-emergency-operation-center-crash-greenacres-south-military-trail-co-pilot-identified-nathalia-filgueiras-florida-news [NTSB]
15. At about 12:47 p.m., at 35,000 ft and in contact with Miami Air Route Traffic Control Center, the crew reported "light clear ice" they said was manageable, plus "moderate turbulence." The turbulence was causing altitude deviations of about 700 ft. — Chapman, Nov 2, per ABC https://abcnews.com/US/learjet-breaking-crashed-florida-ntsb/story?id=137781527, WPTV and CBS12 [NTSB]
16. The crew reported an outside air temperature of −47°C (−53°F). — Chapman, Nov 2, per ABC and CBS12 [NTSB]
17. The crew reported precipitation and requested a climb to 37,000 ft. ATC approved it. — Chapman, Nov 2, per CBS12 and ABC [NTSB]
18. "Less than 30 seconds later, a transmission from the airplane announced the descent, followed by a mayday call." — Chapman, Nov 2, verbatim per WPTV [NTSB]
19. At about 12:48 p.m., a transmission said: "We are in an overspeed condition, descending..." The rest was unintelligible. Later transmissions described a rapid descent and gave altitude callouts. — Chapman, Nov 2, per ABC and CBS12 [NTSB]
20. Impact time: "about 12:51 p.m." — Chapman, per TWC/Yahoo https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/florida-learjet-crash-know-dont-213316401.html and WPTV. "Just before 1 p.m. ET near Military Trail, between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue" — ABC (attributed to Chapman in one summary and to "authorities" in the article text). [NTSB]
21. AIN reported that Chapman said the twinjet crashed "about one minute" after the mayday. https://www.ainonline.com/aviation-news/business-aviation/2026-11-02/investigation-begins-florida-learjet-crash-site [NTSB via MEDIA]
22. Chapman said: "We do have some evidence that there was some breakup of the aircraft before impact" (CBS12). AP/PBS version: "We do have some indication that the aircraft was breaking up before impact" and "We may find parts of the aircraft some distance away from the actual impact site." (Nov 2) https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/evidence-suggests-parts-of-jet-broke-off-before-florida-crash-ntsb-investigator-says (AP, David Fischer and John Funk, Nov 2) [NTSB]
23. Weather is "a major focus of the investigation." Conditions at cruise altitude are not yet determined. Chapman and Monville could not say whether the jet flew through an active thunderstorm. Chapman called the reported turbulence "certainly a factor." — Nov 2, per WPTV, ABC and AP/PBS [NTSB]
24. Chapman said the NTSB does not determine probable cause on scene. He declined to speculate on cause or on the role of either crew member. — Nov 2, per CBS12 and WPTV [NTSB]
25. Chapman said the NTSB is not aware of prior accidents of this type involving the Learjet 60 but will examine trends. — per TWC/Yahoo [NTSB via MEDIA]
26. Investigators will examine what the crew and controllers knew and did, and when. — per CBS12 [NTSB]

## D. Operator / aircraft / occupants
27. Operator: Aurora Aviation dba Upper Jet (NTSB page). Media spell it "Upper Jets". Aviation Week and TWC describe it as Fort Lauderdale-based. [NTSB; MEDIA]
28. Chapman said the flight was operating under **Part 135** (charter). — per AIN (Nov 3) and TWC/Yahoo [NTSB via MEDIA]
29. Nine aboard: 2 crew and 7 passengers. All were killed. — NTSB/Chapman (WPTV, AP/PBS). FAA: "Nine people were on board." [NTSB; FAA]
30. Aircraft age: "22-year-old" — ABC, attributed to FAA (see #12). Not confirmed by the NTSB. The FAA registry MFR year is blank. [MEDIA]
31. **FDR: no source found states whether the aircraft had a flight data recorder.** The NTSB has only discussed the CVR. Do not state either way in the script.
32. CVR: on Nov 2–3, Chapman said maintenance records indicated a CVR was installed, but it was unknown whether it was aboard. It was recovered at the initial impact site and announced Nov 9 (#6). No NTSB statement on the readout or audio quality was found as of Nov 15. — ABC, CBS12, AVweb https://avweb.com/aviation-news/ntsb-recovers-learjet-cockpit-voice-record/ [NTSB]

## E. Wreckage
33. The main wreckage was in an impact crater in the roadway. Rainwater filled it. Monville called recovering it "the biggest obstacle." — Nov 2, per WPTV and AP/PBS [NTSB]
34. Debris field about 0.5 mile by 0.4 mile, covering ball fields, businesses and residences. Some pieces may be farther away. — Monville, Nov 2, per CBS12 and ABC [NTSB]
35. Most of the **left elevator** was found in three pieces, about **1.3 to 3.8 miles** from the crash site. — Chapman, Nov 3 briefing, per Claims Journal/Bloomberg https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/11/04/341109.htm, ABC and Fox [NTSB]
36. Chapman on the elevator: "at least some components, pieces of the aircraft, important pieces of the aircraft, separated before impact" (ABC). "At the speed the aircraft was traveling, I'm not sure it would have been effective" (ABC). "The function of the elevator is to control the pitch up and down" (CBS Miami https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/palm-beach-county-jet-crash-investigation-update/). "Knowing that it was lost is a very important piece of evidence in terms of pointing us towards other things to investigate" (Bloomberg/Claims Journal). It is not yet known whether the loss contributed to the crash or resulted from forces during the descent. [NTSB]
37. Nov 3: the NTSB obtained dashcam footage to help reconstruct the final moments. — Chapman, per Fox https://www.foxnews.com/us/ntsb-finds-no-evidence-mar-a-lago-connection-florida-jet-crash-kills-nine-people [NTSB]
38. Asked about the crash site being less than 10 miles from Mar-a-Lago: "There is no evidence of connection. Purely coincidence." — Chapman, Nov 3, per Fox [NTSB]
39. Team: about 20 investigators on Sunday evening, with more on Monday. Specialists covered aircraft systems, structures, recorders, weather, ATC and drone operations. Parties named: FAA and the Learjet manufacturer. — Nov 2, per WPTV and CBS12 [NTSB]
40. A person on the ground, a man in a vehicle leaving the area next to the site, suffered a leg injury. Its extent is unknown. — Monville, Nov 2, per WPTV, ABC and AP/PBS [NTSB]

## F. Local authorities / on-ground facts
41. About 600 people in the nearby mobile home community were expected to be relocated. — Palm Beach County officials, per WPTV caption, Nov 2 [LOCAL via MEDIA]. Other figures: "hundreds of people from 77 mobile homes" (county officials, per ABC); "almost 80 mobile homes" (AP/PBS); "400 to 600" (WPTV); "roughly 350 to 400 displaced" (Commissioner Joel Flores, Nov 9, per Yahoo/TWC https://www.yahoo.com/news/weather-news/articles/more-pieces-learjet-recovered-investigation-224900674.html). **These numbers conflict.** Use "hundreds of residents".
42. About 50 people stayed overnight at the Greenacres Community Center shelter. A water main break caused outages. — Commissioner Joel Flores, Nov 2, per WPTV https://www.wptv.com/news/palm-beach-county/region-c-palm-beach-county/up-to-600-residents-displaced-by-deadly-learjet-crash-face-uncertain-timeline-for-returning-home [LOCAL]
43. Nov 9: PBSO deputies and forensic specialists "demobilized from the scene." The county extended the local state of emergency, in effect until Nov 15 (Yahoo/TWC). A BocaPost headline says "through Nov. 14". **These dates conflict.** Residents could return starting the night of Nov 9. Flores said all but three homes were deemed safe and water was restored. — Yahoo/TWC; CBS12 https://cbs12.com/news/local/ntsb-cockpit-voice-recorder-found-at-initial-impact-site-in-greenacres-learjet-crash-florida-news-local-news-greenacres-plane-crash-learjet-60-crash [LOCAL via MEDIA]
44. Palm Beach County Fire Rescue said crews spent nine days supporting the site and EOC operations. — per CBS12, Nov 9 [LOCAL]
45. Military Trail was closed near the site (county: between 10th Ave N and Cresthaven Blvd). FDOT said two lanes in each direction were open as of 7:05 p.m. Thu Nov 12, after about 48 hours of paving and striping. — WPTV https://www.wptv.com/news/palm-beach-county/region-c-palm-beach-county/military-trail-reopens-in-both-directions-after-repairs-following-deadly-learjet-crash [LOCAL via MEDIA]
46. The medical examiner is running a "lengthy process" of DNA identification. PBSO said it was too early to release identities. — ABC [LOCAL]
47. Gov. Ron DeSantis (Nov 1): "Obviously, this was a cataclysmic plane crash." "It looked like a missile going down. It left a big crater on the side of the road." — ABC Nov 1 [OFFICIAL]
48. Crash location: near S. Military Trail between Cresthaven Blvd and 10th Ave N, Greenacres area, Palm Beach County (NTSB/CBS12, ABC). Next to the Green Acres Mobile Home Community. John I. Leonard High School is among the NTSB-cleared areas (#4). No official latitude/longitude was found. The Wikipedia coordinates are uncited, so don't use them. The FAA used "near Cresthaven". AP said about 7 miles southwest of West Palm Beach.

## H. Later developments to Nov 15
50. Nov 9: CVR recovered (#6). Nov 12: Military Trail reopened (#45). As of Nov 15, no NTSB statement on CVR content was found, and no preliminary report, FAA action against the operator, or certificate change was found.
51. Preliminary report: no NTSB statement giving a date was found. Standard NTSB practice is a preliminary report usually within about 30 days and a final report typically 12–24 months later. That is general practice, not a statement about this case. Phrase it as "typically".

## I. NOT official — avoid or label clearly
- ATC audio (LiveATC.net) and crew radio quotes reported by AP, Fox and CNN: "We're going to crash into the ground", the expletive in Fox's timeline, crew-stated descent rates of "10,000" and "22,000 fpm, 60° bank", and attributing specific radio calls to a named crew member. Use NTSB paraphrases instead (#15–19).
- Fox timeline https://www.foxnews.com/us/timeline-learjet-crash-killed-austin-family-traces-final-minutes-takeoff-florida-plunge: ~41,000 ft cruise, the 12:31–12:40 ATC weather/routing exchanges, the 12:36 descent to 35,000, a peak of 35,450 ft, ~10,000→20,000 fpm, and a crash at 12:49. These come from ATC audio and flight tracking, not the NTSB. **41,000 ft cruise is NOT confirmed by the NTSB.** The NTSB said only that the aircraft was at 35,000 ft at 12:47.
- FlightRadar24 figures (">32,000 fpm", storm-cell radar overlays) and the "10,000 to 30,000 ft/min" in AP's Nov 1 story (from flight data or ATC). Don't use them.
- "26,000 feet in about a minute": derived from ABC's FAA-attributed 35,250→8,750 ft (#12). No NTSB statement. If used, attribute it to "ABC News, citing the FAA".
- "350 feet per second": ABC's own arithmetic.
- Expert opinions: Alan Diehl (Fox), Jeff Guzzetti (AP), Substack and YouTube analyses. These are not official.
- The debris "across roughly two miles": FL State Rep. Debra Tendrich (WPTV, Nov 2). It differs from the NTSB's 0.5 × 0.4 mile debris field. The elevator pieces were 1.3–3.8 miles away.
- Victim and crew identities: these come from families, a PR firm and media. Authorities had not confirmed them in early reporting. Do not name anyone.
- The attribution of the "very important piece of evidence" quote conflicts. Bloomberg/Claims Journal: about the elevator. Fox: about the engine part. Use the Bloomberg version, or avoid the quote.

## J. Conflicts summary
- Crash time: NTSB/Chapman ~12:51 p.m. EST. FAA ~12:50 p.m. local. Fox timeline 12:49 (tracking). ABC "just before 1 p.m."
- Cruise altitude: 35,000 ft at the time of the reports (NTSB) vs ~41,000 ft earlier (ATC audio and tracking only).
- Descent: no NTSB number. ABC/FAA 35,250→8,750 ft in "a little over a minute". Media rates range from 10k to 32k fpm.
- Displaced residents: 77 / almost 80 homes; 350–400 / 400–600 / up to 600 people.
- Local emergency end date: Nov 14 vs Nov 15.
- Operator name: "Upper Jet" (NTSB) vs "Upper Jets" (media).
