NTSB PROBABLE CAUSE · ADOPTED JUNE 24, 2014
“…the flight crew’s mismanagement of the airplane’s descent during the visual approach, the pilot flying’s unintended deactivation of automatic airspeed control, the flight crew’s inadequate monitoring of airspeed, and the flight crew’s delayed execution of a go-around after they became aware that the airplane was below acceptable glidepath and airspeed tolerances.”
CONTRIBUTING TO THE ACCIDENT · NTSB
1 Complexities of the autothrottle and autopilot flight director systems, inadequately described in Boeing’s documentation and Asiana’s pilot training → more likely mode error
2 The flight crew’s nonstandard communication and coordination on the A/T and AFDS
3 The pilot flying’s inadequate training on planning and executing visual approaches
4 The pilot monitoring/instructor pilot’s inadequate supervision of the pilot flying
5 Flight crew fatigue, which likely degraded their performance
NTSB SAFETY RECOMMENDATIONS A-14-37 … A-14-63 (27)

FAA Better 777 autothrottle training · demo of when A/T gives no low-speed protection · design review (A-14-37…42)
FAA Design requirements for low energy alerting (A-14-43) · BOEING wide-body autoflight change (A-14-57)
ASIANA MCP callouts · supervised new instructors · more manual flight (A-14-52, 53, 55)
ARFF Minimum staffing for interior rescue · training for officers in command (A-14-48, 49, 60, 61)
